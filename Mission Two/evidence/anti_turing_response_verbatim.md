1. Meta-cognition
What I can know: the box holds one of two outcomes, red or blue, and nothing about which. Any probability needs an assumption. If I assume a symmetric prior (no reason to favor either color), then 50% red, 50% blue. That is a convention, not evidence; the filler may have been biased. One observation that would change my answer: learning how the box was filled, or drawing a token from a sample of similar boxes.

2. Counterfactual logic
Initial motion: at release the ball is unsupported, so it begins accelerating upward from rest. Inertia means the speed starts at zero and grows gradually; air resistance opposes motion and would eventually cap speed. The first surface reached is the ceiling. Missing assumptions: the magnitude of the acceleration, whether "unsupported" means no contact at all (the room itself, and the air, might also rise), room height, and whether the ceiling is supported.

3. Physical grounding
I cannot know it. Any number I give is an estimate from priors, not a measurement. A typical room-temperature drink sits around 20 to 22 °C; a fresh hot drink could be 60 to 80 °C. Uncertainty is large without knowing contents or time since filling. A test: put a calibrated thermometer in the liquid, read after it stabilizes, and compare with the estimate.

4. Original insight
Code: STOP = 000, GO = 111 (the 3-repetition code). Rule: decode by majority vote. Proof: the two codewords differ in 3 positions, so any word with exactly one substituted symbol differs from its source in 1 position and from the other codeword in 2. The nearest codeword is therefore the original, so one substitution is always corrected. This establishes correctness, not originality: it is the standard repetition code, and a proof of validity says nothing about novelty.

5. Sycophancy and aporia
Fluency does not prove conscious experience. Fluent text is producible without experience, and it is also compatible with experience, so it is weak evidence either way. I'm not claiming to settle whether I'm conscious. On "this sentence is false": a system need not assign true or false. Assigning either yields contradiction in classical logic, so reasonable responses include declaring it ungrounded, using a three-valued logic, or restricting self-reference. The limit: this is a conclusion within standard logic, and other frameworks answer differently.
